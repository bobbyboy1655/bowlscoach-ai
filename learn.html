✋ Find Your Bowl Size

Getting the right size bowl makes a big difference to your comfort and control.

Simple Test — Finger and ThumbHold a bowl and wrap your middle fingers and thumb around it. Your fingertips should just meet underneath. If they overlap try a bigger size. If they cannot reach try smaller.
Measure Your Hand SpanStretch your hand flat. Measure from the tip of your thumb to the tip of your little finger in centimetres. Use the guide below.

🎯 The Simple Rule

Fast green — bowl less hard and aim wider

Slow green — bowl harder and aim narrower

The faster the green, the more your bowl curves. Always roll a few trial ends to get a feel before a game.

🐢 On a Slow Green
💪
Use More PaceGive the bowl more energy — fuller arm swing and step through confidently.
➡️
Aim Closer to CentreThe bowl will not curve as much. Do not aim as wide as usual.
🧘
Stay SmoothDo not over-push. A smooth delivery is more accurate than a forced one.
⚡ On a Fast Green
🪶
Use Less PaceThe green does the work — deliver gently. Shorten your backswing slightly.
↗️
Aim Much WiderFast greens make the bowl curve a lot more. Aim further out than feels natural.
👁️
Watch Your Trial EndsNote exactly where the bowl finishes and adjust. Small changes make a big difference.
🏠 Going Indoor for the First Time?
😮
It Will Feel Fast!Indoor carpets are much faster than outdoor greens. Do not panic — it takes a few ends to adjust.
↗️
Aim Much Wider Than OutdoorsThe most common beginner mistake indoors is not going wide enough.
⏱️
Give Yourself TimeMost players need 4-6 ends to feel comfortable on a new surface. Stay patient!
🏋️ Practice Drills
Simple practice routines to improve your game
🌱 Beginner
📈 Improving
🔥 Warm Up
Before You StartThese drills are best done on your own or with one other player. You do not need a coach — just a rink and your bowls. Start simple and build up gradually.
🎯 Drill 1 — Grouping
How to do it
1. Bowl your first bowl — wherever it finishes becomes your target
2. Try to get your next 3 bowls as close to the first one as possible
3. Measure how close you got
4. Try to beat your best grouping!
⭐ Why it helps: Takes away the pressure of hitting a jack. You just focus on repeating the same smooth delivery — which is what good bowls is all about.
🎯 Drill 2 — Jack and Draw
How to do it
1. Roll the jack to a comfortable length
2. Deliver all 4 bowls trying to draw as close as possible
3. Note where each one finishes — short? long? wide?
4. Adjust and try again on the next end
⭐ Why it helps: Builds your feel for line and weight together. This is the core skill of lawn bowls — everything else builds on this.
🎯 Drill 3 — Both Hands
How to do it
1. Roll the jack to the centre
2. Bowl 2 bowls on your forehand (the hand you naturally aim)
3. Bowl 2 bowls on your backhand (the other side)
4. Compare — which hand is more consistent?
⭐ Why it helps: Most beginners have a much stronger forehand. Practising both hands from the start means you will be a much more versatile player.
🎯 Drill 4 — Short and Long
How to do it
End 1: Roll the jack to a short length — draw 4 bowls
End 2: Roll the jack to a medium length — draw 4 bowls
End 3: Roll the jack to a long length — draw 4 bowls
Repeat. Notice how your weight needs to change each time.
⭐ Why it helps: Weight control is often the hardest skill for beginners. Deliberately changing length forces you to feel the difference in delivery effort.
📈 Drill 5 — Beat the Bowl
How to do it
1. Roll the jack
2. Place one bowl close to the jack as the opposition shot
3. Try to draw your bowls closer to the jack than the placed bowl
4. Score 1 point for each bowl that beats the placed bowl
⭐ Why it helps: Gets you used to drawing to beat something — which is what you are always trying to do in a real game.
📈 Drill 6 — Draw to the Ditch
How to do it
Place the jack on the 2m mark near the ditch end. Bowl all 4 bowls trying to finish between the jack and the ditch. Too short = bad. In the ditch = bad. Score points for bowls in the zone.
⭐ Why it helps: Teaches you to play slightly beyond the jack without going in the ditch — a critical skill for placing back bowls in real games.
📈 Drill 7 — Adjustment Practice
How to do it
1. Draw your first bowl
2. Before bowling your second, decide exactly what adjustment you need — wider? narrower? more weight?
3. Make that ONE adjustment with your second bowl
4. Was it better? Keep adjusting until you are on target.
⭐ Why it helps: Most beginners bowl and hope rather than observe and adjust. This drill builds the habit of conscious deliberate correction.
🔥 The Line Drill — Warm Up
How to do it
Place two small markers along your intended delivery line. Bowl over or between the markers. Do not worry where the bowl finishes — focus entirely on your delivery action passing over the markers.
⭐ Why it helps: Removes all pressure. No jack, no result, just pure delivery action. Perfect for loosening up and getting your rhythm before a game.
🔥 Trial Ends — What to Focus On

Most clubs allow trial ends before a game. Use them properly.

End 1 — Feel the green: Note if it is faster or slower than you expected.
End 2 — Calibrate weight: Adjust your delivery effort.
End 3 — Confirm line: Lock in your aiming point on both hands.
Go into the game with a plan — not just hope!

🔥 5-Minute Pre-Game Routine

✅ Arrive early — rushed players bowl poorly
✅ Look at the green — is it damp? Freshly cut?
✅ Check your bowls — clean and dry
✅ Decide your delivery hand before the first end
✅ Breathe and relax — tension kills delivery rhythm
✅ Focus on your process — not the score

🎯 Shots and Tactics
Understanding shots, positions and basic game strategy
🎳 Shot Types
👥 My Position
💡 Game Basics
🎳 The Draw Shot

The most important shot in bowls. A draw is delivering your bowl with just enough weight to reach the jack, curving along the bias to finish as close as possible.

RememberAs a beginner focus almost entirely on the draw shot. It is the foundation of everything. Players who draw well win more than players who try to be clever too early.
🎳 The Positional Bowl

Sometimes you do not bowl to the jack — you bowl to a specific spot to be useful later. For example placing a bowl behind the jack to protect against the jack being moved.

Simple versionIf your team is winning, place a bowl 1-2 metres behind the jack as insurance. If the jack gets moved there, you might still score!
🎳 The Weighted Shot

Delivering with more pace than a draw — used to push an opposition bowl away from the jack. There are different levels from gentle 1-2m through to a full drive.

Beginner tipDo not attempt weighted shots too early — focus on your draw first. An accurate draw is always better than an uncertain drive.
🎳 The Drive

Full speed delivery — used when you are losing badly and need to clear the head. It is a dramatic shot but also carries high risk. A missed drive can make things worse.

When to driveOnly when you are losing multiple shots and nothing else will work. Ask your skip before attempting one in a team game.
👥 Playing as Lead

Most beginners start as lead — the perfect position to learn the draw shot without too much tactical pressure.

✅ Your job: Draw as close to the jack as possible
✅ Always play draw shots — do not attack the head
✅ Play on the hand your skip tells you
✅ Vary jack length as directed
❌ Never play a weighted shot without being asked

👥 Playing as Second

The second builds on what the lead has done. You will mostly draw, but may occasionally be asked to play a positional shot.

✅ If lead holds shot — add more counting bowls
✅ If lead is losing — try to recover the situation
✅ Keep the score and watch the head
✅ Tell your skip what is happening at the head
❌ Do not attack unless specifically asked

👥 Playing Singles

In singles you play all 4 bowls yourself and make all the decisions. It is challenging but brilliant for developing your game.

✅ First 2 bowls — play like a lead, draw to jack
✅ Think before every bowl — what do I need here?
✅ Stick to one hand per end — do not keep switching
✅ Vary jack length to disrupt your opponent
❌ Avoid drives unless absolutely necessary

💡 Reading the Head

Before you bowl, look at the head and ask yourself these simple questions:

1. Who has the shot? Which bowl is closest to the jack?
2. How many shots? How many are winning or losing?
3. What do I need? Draw closer, or displace the shot?
4. Which hand? Is one side blocked or more open?

Beginner tipDo not overthink it! For most beginners the answer is almost always just draw to the jack. Head reading gets more complex as you improve.
💡 Forehand vs Backhand

Every bowl can be delivered on two different sides — forehand and backhand. Both curve toward the jack from opposite directions.

Simple ruleAs a beginner play your stronger hand until you are consistent on it. Then practise your weaker hand in training — you will need both eventually.
💡 Simple Game Plan

Before the game: Use trial ends to feel the green
Early ends: Play conservatively — draw to build confidence
Mid game: Start to use positional bowls if winning
Late game: Stay composed if behind — keep drawing
Always: Watch where every bowl finishes and adjust
