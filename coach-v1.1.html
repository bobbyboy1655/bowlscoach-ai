📏 How to Measure
🎳
Standard Method
Deliver a bowl with normal draw weight. Time from release until it stops. Average 3 readings. That is your green speed in seconds.
Coaching TipGreen speed changes during the day. Measure before sessions and note conditions to build a picture over the season.
📊 Speed Scale

Higher seconds means faster green. The bowl rolls further for the same delivery effort.

🌿 Factors Affecting Speed
✂️
Grass Length
The biggest factor. 1mm difference can change speed by several seconds.
Shorter = Faster
💧
Moisture
Wet greens are significantly slower. Greens speed up as they dry during the day.
Wet = Slower
☀️
Temperature
Hot dry conditions firm the surface. Why Australian greens are naturally faster.
Hot and Dry = Faster
🔄
Rolling
Regular rolling compacts the surface and increases speed.
Regular Rolling = Faster
🎯 The Key Principle

Faster green means less weight and wider line.
Slower green means more weight and narrower line.

Bias effect increases significantly on faster greens.

🐢 Slow Green 9-12 seconds
💪
More WeightFuller arm swing and positive step-through. The green gives less momentum.
➡️
Narrow the LineLess bias effect on slow surfaces. Most players do not narrow enough.
🎳
Bowl ChoiceWider-biased bowl compensates — XP, Legacy SL, Tiger EVO, Sonic.
⚡ Fast Green 14-17 seconds
🪶
Less WeightShorten backswing. Small errors are amplified on fast surfaces.
↗️
Wider LineBias effect increases dramatically. Most players do not go wide enough.
🎳
Bowl ChoiceNarrower bias — d-tec, Vector VS, Tiger TX, Quantum.
🏠 Indoor vs Outdoor
📉
Going Outdoor After IndoorSignificant slow-down. Be patient and let the green show what it needs.
📈
Going Indoor After OutdoorLightning fast by comparison. Widen line dramatically. Allow 4-6 ends to calibrate.
📚 Drills Library
World Bowls endorsed coaching drills — beginner to advanced
🌱 Basic
📈 Progressive
🎮 Games
⚡ Circuit
How to UseRun these in coaching sessions. Vary mat position — short, medium and long. Record results for tracking progress.
🎯 Skill 1 — Jack Delivery and First Bowl Effect
Setup
Player delivers jack to target mark then delivers bowl to finish as close as possible to the jack.
💡 Coach Focus
Assess pre-shot routine, mat approach, delivery action and follow through. Check first bowl effectiveness — correct hand and line from the start?
🎯 Skill 2 — Shot Recovery
Setup
Place an opposition bowl close to the jack. Player must draw to beat it — finish closer to the jack.
💡 Coach Focus
Does the player assess the head before delivering? Correct hand? Is weight control sufficient to just beat the shot?
🎯 Skill 3 — Draw Off Line
Setup
Place jack close to rink boundary at 25m. Player draws two bowls forehand and two backhand to the jack.
💡 Coach Focus
Can the player adjust their line for an off-centre jack? This drill exposes hand weakness quickly.
🎯 Skill 4 — Grouping No Jack
Setup
No jack. Player bowls first bowl — it becomes the target. Next three bowls delivered to group as close as possible.
💡 Coach Focus
Removes mental pressure of a jack. Focus shifts to replicating the same delivery. Reveals line and length consistency issues.
🎯 Skill 5 — Draw to the Ditch
Setup
Place jack on 2m mark. All four bowls must finish between the jack and the ditch.
💡 Coach Focus
Excellent weight control drill. Brilliant for skips who need consistent back bowl placement.
🎳 Warm Up — Delivery Line Drill
Setup
Place two discs along the intended delivery line. Player bowls over or between the discs. Outcome is NOT the priority.
💡 Coach Focus
Assess pre-shot routine, mat approach, delivery action and follow through. One of the few drills where the result does not matter.
📈 Progressive Drawing Drill

3 players per rink, 2 bowls each. All draw to a placed jack. Closest bowl moves up one rink. Furthest moves down. Run for 15 ends.

💡 Coach Value
Identifies consistently accurate players. Exposes hand weaknesses. Creates healthy competitive pressure in training.
📈 40 Bowl Skills Assessment

10 ends, 4 bowls per end. Ends 1-5: 2 forehand then 2 backhand. Ends 6-10: reverse. Score each bowl: E=Effective S=Short L=Long W=Wide N=Narrow.

💡 Coach Value
Comprehensive snapshot of a player's all-round game. Run at regular intervals to track improvement over a season.
📈 Short Bowl Penalty Game

Normal game. Any bowl finishing more than 30cm short of the jack is removed immediately.

💡 Coach Value
Quickly eliminates the habit of consistently bowling short. Creates real accountability in a game-like context.
📈 Line and Speed Adjustment

Player draws to target X. Coach repositions the target. Player must make the necessary adjustments with their next bowl.

💡 Coach Value
Tests the most critical in-game skill — the ability to adjust after seeing where your bowl finishes.
🎮 Rotating Positions Game

Fours game, 4 ends per set. Each player starts with 4 bowls reducing by one each end. After 4 ends all players rotate position. Then 3, then 2, then 1 bowl.

💡 Coach Value
Builds empathy across positions. Players understand what each role needs. Excellent for building team cohesion.
🎮 Phantom Bowler

Coach places opposition bowls on the head to create specific tactical situations. Team plays normally against the placed bowls.

💡 Coach Value
The most powerful tactical coaching tool available. Coach controls every scenario. Ask questions to develop player thinking.
🎮 Super Challenge

Squad in two teams. Each player plays ONE bowl over TWO ends to find a winner. Closest counting bowls win the set.

💡 Coach Value
High pressure single bowl situations. Excellent for developing composure under competitive pressure.
⚡ 8-Station Circuit

World Bowls endorsed. Players keep individual scores and move between stations. 8 rinks required.

Station 1 — Jack Delivery Accuracy
8 jacks. Deliver 2 to each of 4 markers at different lengths. Score jacks landing within the target zone.
Station 2 — Ditch Control
Score only jacks finishing between the ditch and the 2m mark.
Station 3 — Line Taking
Bowl between markers. Gradually narrow until bowling only over a discus.
Station 4 — Weight and Centre Return
2m mat, forehand and backhand. Score bowls returning to within 30cm either side of centre.
Station 5 — 2m Zone Play
Place cones 1m either side of jack. All bowls must finish between cone and jack.
Station 6 — Playing Around a Bowl
Draw around a wing bowl to jack. Draw between jack and bowl 2m behind. Repeat on other hand.
Station 7 — Target Zone
Cones 30cm either side of centre. Draw into zone without touching cones. 2 backhand, 2 forehand.
Station 8 — Mixed Shots
1. Firm force 2 bowls backhand. 2. Controlled force 2 bowls forehand. 3. Drive one, draw to off-line jack.
🎯 Shot Selection and Tactics
Shot types, positions, head reading and match strategy
🎳 Shots
👥 Positions
🧠 Head Reading
♟️ Strategy
🎳 The Draw Shot

The foundation of all lawn bowls. Deliver with just enough weight to reach the jack on the correct line.

When to useAlways the first choice. Draw to build the head before considering weighted shots.
Coach tipConsistency over distance. A player who draws well wins more than one who drives well.
🎳 The Positional Shot

A draw played to a specific position — not necessarily to the jack. Used to cover, block or protect.

When to useHolding shot — place a bowl 1-2m behind the jack. Losing — cover potential jack movement.
Coach tipMost club players do not play positional bowls enough. Teach them to think 2-3 bowls ahead.
🎳 Controlled Weight 1-2m Through

Extra weight to push a bowl 1-2m through the jack area without killing the end.

When to useTo remove a shot bowl without disturbing the whole head. Precision over power.
Coach tipThe hardest shot to teach. Focus on smooth delivery. Player must commit to a specific weight.
🎳 The Drive

Full pace delivery. Used to kill the end, remove multiple bowls or drive the jack into the ditch.

When to useLast resort when down multiple shots. Use sparingly.
Coach tipMust be practised constantly. A poor drive is worse than not driving. It is a tool of last resort not a panic shot.
👥 The Lead
Primary Role
Sets up the head. Draw as close as possible to the jack on both hands.

Key qualities: Consistent draw shot, strong on both hands, good first bowl effectiveness. Never attack the head — always draw.

👥 The Second
Primary Role
Builds on the lead's work. If lead holds shot, consolidate. If lead is losing, recover the situation.

Key qualities: Versatile all-rounder, draws and positional shots, records the score. Should never attack the head without instruction from the skip.

👥 The Third
Primary Role
Gets the skip into the best possible position. Must draw, play weighted shots, and read the head well enough to advise the skip accurately.

Key qualities: Sound tactical awareness, accurate head reading, good communication. Unsolicited advice to the skip should be avoided.

👥 The Skip
Primary Role
Leads the team tactically, technically and emotionally. Sets jack lengths, directs shot selection for all team members.

Key qualities: Master of all shots, strong tactical mind, excellent head reading, composure under pressure.

Common Skip ErrorBecoming unreceptive to team input. The best skips consult their third and remain open to tactical discussion.
🧠 The 5 Questions

Before calling a shot:

1. Who holds the shot?
2. How many shots?
3. Where is the danger?
4. What shot is available?
5. What is the risk vs reward?

🧠 Reading the Back

Many players focus only on bowls near the jack and miss critical back bowls. Always assess which team has back bowls and where, if the jack moves who benefits, and whether the opponent has a trap bowl that scores multiple shots if the jack moves there.

🧠 Shot or Jack Situation
If you own the shot
Place back bowls behind the jack in catching positions. Cover any re-spot positions to protect what you have.
If opposition holds the shot
Direct your lead to get a bowl into the head. First bowl 1-2m behind jack, second bowl jack-high to create a larger target.
♟️ Jack Length Strategy

Play long when you are stronger at long ends or green is fast.

Play short when your team is stronger short or opposition lead dominates long ends.

Vary the length — unpredictability prevents the opposition settling into a pattern.

♟️ Team Communication

Role clarity wins games — clear expectations, players trust each other to deliver.

Simple communication — clear concise instructions from skip. Complex tactical discussions belong in the changing room.

Encouragement over criticism — a quiet supportive word is worth more than frustration.

♟️ Singles Strategy

Opening bowls — play like a lead, draw shots to build early position.

Third bowl is critical — your most tactically important bowl. Consolidate or recover.

Avoid unnecessary drives — every aggressive shot risks scattering your own bowls.

Singles MindsetYou must fulfil all four team roles yourself. Assess the head before every bowl as if you were the skip — because you are.
