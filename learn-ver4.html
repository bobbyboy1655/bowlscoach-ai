⏱️ What Speed Is My Green?

Green speed is measured in seconds. Higher number means faster green.

9s Slow12s14s17s20s Fast
12
seconds
📏 How to Measure Before a Game

Step 1: Deliver a bowl with your normal draw weight.
Step 2: Time from the moment you release until the bowl stops.
Step 3: Average 3 readings — that is your green speed in seconds.
Step 4: Note conditions — morning dew makes greens slower.

🌿 The Simple Rule

Fast green — bowl less hard and aim wider

Slow green — bowl harder and aim narrower

The faster the green, the more your bowl curves.

🐢 On a Slow Green
💪
Use More PaceFuller arm swing and step through confidently.
➡️
Aim Closer to CentreThe bowl will not curve as much as usual.
⚡ On a Fast Green
🪶
Use Less PaceThe green does the work — deliver gently.
↗️
Aim Much WiderFast greens make the bowl curve a lot more.
🏠 Going Indoor for the First Time?
😮
It Will Feel Fast!Indoor carpets are much faster. Give yourself 4-6 ends to adjust.
↗️
Aim Much Wider Than OutdoorsThe most common beginner mistake indoors.
👁️ Finding Your Line

Every bowler needs a consistent aiming point — a fixed reference to aim at when delivering. Here is how to find and use yours.

Step 1 — Use the Rink Boundary Marker
The rink has boundary markers along the side. Pick the marker closest to where you want your bowl to travel at its widest point. This becomes your aiming point.
Step 2 — End of Rink Objects
Look beyond the head to fixed objects at the end of the rink — a post, a window, a mark on the bank. Line yourself up to that fixed object on each delivery.
Step 3 — Adjust In or Out
If your bowl went wide — aim slightly more towards the centre next time. If it fell short of the jack — aim slightly further out. Small adjustments only.
ImportantNever use mid-green marks like grass discolouration or carpet patterns as aiming points — they change during the game. Always use fixed end-of-rink references.

Simple practice routines to improve your game

🔥 Warm Up
Before You StartThese drills work best on your own or with one other player. Start simple and build gradually.
⭐ Drill 1 — Grouping
How to do it
1. Bowl your first bowl — wherever it stops is your target
2. Try to get your next 3 bowls as close to the first as possible
3. Measure how close you got
4. Try to beat your best grouping!
⭐ Why it helps: Removes the pressure of a jack. Focus on repeating the same smooth delivery — the core skill in bowls.
⭐ Drill 2 — Jack and Draw
How to do it
1. Roll the jack to a comfortable length
2. Deliver all 4 bowls as close as possible
3. Note where each finishes — short? long? wide?
4. Adjust and try again
⭐ Why it helps: Builds feel for line and weight together — the core skill of lawn bowls.
⭐ Drill 3 — Both Hands
How to do it
1. Roll the jack to the centre
2. Bowl 2 bowls forehand, 2 bowls backhand
3. Compare — which hand is more consistent?
⭐ Why it helps: Most beginners have a much stronger forehand. Practising both from the start makes you a more versatile player.
⭐ Drill 4 — Short and Long
How to do it
End 1: Short jack — draw 4 bowls
End 2: Medium jack — draw 4 bowls
End 3: Long jack — draw 4 bowls
Notice how your weight changes each time.
⭐ Why it helps: Weight control is the hardest skill for beginners. Changing length forces you to feel the difference in effort.
📈 Drill 5 — Beat the Bowl
How to do it
1. Roll the jack
2. Place one bowl close to the jack as opposition
3. Try to draw your bowls closer than the placed bowl
4. Score 1 point for each bowl that beats it
⭐ Why it helps: Gets you used to drawing to beat something — what you are always trying to do in a real game.
📈 Drill 6 — Draw to the Ditch
How to do it
Place jack on the 2m mark. Bowl all 4 trying to finish between the jack and the ditch. Too short = bad. In the ditch = bad.
⭐ Why it helps: Teaches you to play past the jack without going in the ditch — critical for back bowl placement.
📈 Drill 7 — Adjustment Practice
How to do it
1. Draw your first bowl
2. Decide exactly what adjustment you need
3. Make that ONE adjustment with your second bowl
4. Was it better? Keep adjusting.
⭐ Why it helps: Builds the habit of conscious deliberate correction instead of bowling and hoping.
Weighted Shot DrillsThese drills develop controlled weight — from a gentle yard-on through to a full drive. Master draw first before attempting these.
💪 Yard-On Weight
A yard-on shot travels 1-2 metres past the jack. Used to nudge an opposition bowl away without disturbing the whole head.
Practice: Set up a target bowl 30cm from the jack. Deliver with just enough extra weight to move it 1m while your bowl stays in the head area.
🔴 Jack Trailing
Jack trailing means hitting the jack with your bowl so that it moves toward your back bowls, scoring multiple shots. A highly skilled shot used by skips.
Practice: Place jack at 23m. Bowl with controlled drive weight directly at the jack. Note how far it trails. Build consistency before using in a game.
💥 Knock-On into Head
A knock-on uses a deflection off one bowl to reach another or the jack. Requires precise weight and line. Used by experienced players to navigate a cluttered head.
Practice with spring-loaded target sets available from bowls retailers — excellent for developing yard-on weight consistency without disturbing a live head.
🔲 The Australian Grid Game

A brilliant draw skill game from Australia. The jack can be placed anywhere within a 3x3 grid at the head end. Points are scored for the nearest bowl inside the grid, with penalties for bowls outside the boundary or in the ditch.

How to Play
1. Place the grid (9 squares, approx 1m x 1m) at the head end
2. Place the jack anywhere within the grid
3. Players draw all bowls to finish as close to the jack as possible
4. Score: Inside grid nearest jack = +3, next nearest = +2, in grid = +1
5. Outside grid boundary = -1, in the ditch = -2
6. Move the jack to a different grid square each end
⭐ Why it's great: Develops all-round draw skill, forces line adjustment as the jack position varies, and adds a scoring system that makes practice competitive and fun.
🔥 The Line Drill
How to do it
Place two small markers along your intended delivery line. Bowl over or between the markers. Do not worry where the bowl finishes — focus entirely on your delivery passing over the markers.
⭐ Removes all pressure. No jack, no result, just pure delivery action. Perfect for loosening up before a game.
🔥 Trial Ends — Use Them Properly

End 1: Feel the green — faster or slower than expected?
End 2: Calibrate weight — adjust your delivery effort.
End 3: Confirm line — lock in your aiming point both hands.
Go into the game with a plan!

💡 Game Basics
🗣️ Communication
🔴 The Draw Shot

The most important shot in bowls. Deliver with just enough weight to reach the jack, curving along the bias to finish as close as possible.

RememberAs a beginner focus almost entirely on the draw shot. Players who draw well win more than players who try to be clever too early.
🔴 The Positional Bowl

Sometimes you bowl to a specific spot rather than the jack — to cover, protect or block. For example placing a bowl 1-2m behind the jack as insurance if the jack moves.

Simple versionIf your team is winning, place a bowl behind the jack. If the jack gets moved there, you might still score!
🔴 The Weighted Shot

More pace than a draw — used to push opposition bowls away. Ranges from a gentle yard-on through to a full drive. Jack trailing is a skilled weighted shot where you hit the jack to move it to your back bowls.

Beginner tipMaster your draw first. An accurate draw is always better than an uncertain drive.
🔴 The Drive

Full speed delivery. Used when losing badly and nothing else will work. High risk — a missed drive can make things worse.

When to driveOnly when losing multiple shots and nothing else will work. Ask your skip before attempting one in a team game.
👥 Playing as Lead

✅ Draw as close to the jack as possible
✅ Always play draw shots
✅ Play on the hand your skip tells you
✅ Collect the bowls at the end if your team loses
❌ Never play a weighted shot without being asked

👥 Playing as Second

✅ Build on what the lead has done
✅ Keep the score and watch the head
✅ Tell your skip what is happening at the head
✅ Draw or play positional as directed
❌ Do not attack unless specifically asked

👥 Playing as Skip

✅ Read the head before every bowl
✅ Give clear concise instructions to your team
✅ Set the jack length each end
✅ Be the calm positive presence for your team
✅ Consult your third on close decisions

👥 Playing Singles

✅ First 2 bowls — play like a lead, draw to jack
✅ Think before every bowl
✅ Vary jack length to disrupt your opponent
✅ Stay composed if behind
❌ Avoid drives unless absolutely necessary

💡 Reading the Head

1. Who has the shot? Which bowl is closest?
2. How many shots? Winning or losing?
3. What do I need? Draw or displace?
4. Which hand? Is one side blocked?

Beginner tipFor most beginners the answer is almost always just draw to the jack. Head reading gets more complex as you improve.
💡 Simple Game Plan

Before the game: Use trial ends to feel the green
Early ends: Draw conservatively to build confidence
Mid game: Use positional bowls if winning
Late game: Stay composed if behind — keep drawing
Always: Watch where every bowl finishes and adjust

🗣️ Skip to Team Communication

Good communication wins games. The skip's instructions must be precise and achievable.

What a good skip says
"Forehand draw, just past the jack" ✅
"Backhand, draw to the shot bowl" ✅
"Play the forehand, yard on weight" ✅
What a good skip avoids
"Just get it somewhere near" ❌
Sighing or showing frustration ❌
Changing instructions mid-delivery ❌
🗣️ Second to Skip

The second stands at the head with the skip and provides accurate information:

"We hold shot by a foot"
"They have two, we need to draw inside"
"Jack is close to the ditch"

The second should only offer tactical opinions when asked by the skip.

🤝 Courtesy and Etiquette
🤝
Before the Game
Shake hands with all opponents before the game begins. Introduce yourself. Wish them a good game.
🔴
Trial Ends
When skips go for their final trial bowls before the game, all other players stand still and quiet at the side of the rink.
🏆
After the Game
Shake hands again. Thank your opponents regardless of the result. Acknowledge good shots by both teams during the game.
🤫
During Play
Stay quiet while opponents deliver. Stand still. Do not distract. Move to the correct position — behind the mat or behind the head.

🌐 Rule Books
📋 Essential Rules
🔴
The MatThe mat must be placed centrally on the centre line. The front edge must be at least 2 metres from the ditch. Players must have one foot on or over the mat when delivering.
⚪
The JackThe jack must travel at least 23 metres from the mat and finish on the rink. If it goes in the ditch or out of bounds on the first delivery, the opponent delivers. If out again, jack is placed at 2m from the far ditch.
🟡
Delivering Your BowlBowls must be delivered from the mat. If a bowl goes out of the side boundary it is dead and removed. If it finishes in the ditch it is dead unless it touched the jack.
💧
The DitchBowls in the ditch are dead unless they are touchers — bowls that touched the jack during their travel. Touchers are marked with chalk and remain live even in the ditch.
📏
MeasuringWhen it is not clear which bowl is nearest the jack, a measure is taken. The skip or third normally measures. Both teams must agree on the result.
🏆 How Scoring Works
🏆
Counting ShotsAt the end of each end, the team with the bowl nearest the jack scores one point for every bowl that is closer to the jack than the nearest opposition bowl.
0️⃣
Dead EndIf the jack is driven out of bounds or into the ditch (without being a valid toucher situation), the end is declared dead and replayed. No score is recorded.
🏅
WinningIn singles, first to 21 shots wins. In team games, the number of ends is usually pre-agreed (typically 18-21 ends). The team with the most shots at the end wins.
🤝
Tied GameIf scores are level after the agreed number of ends, an extra end is played to decide the winner.
🤝 Etiquette Guide
🤝
HandshakesAlways shake hands with all opponents before and after the game. This is one of the most important traditions in bowls.
👏
Acknowledge Good ShotsApplaud good shots by both teams. A quiet word of appreciation to an opponent who plays well is always welcomed.
🤫
Stay Quiet During DeliveryDo not talk, move around or distract players while they are delivering. Stand still behind the mat or behind the head.
📱
Mobile PhonesKeep phones on silent during play. Step away from the rink if you need to take a call.
⏱️
Pace of PlayBe ready to bowl when it is your turn. Do not slow the game unnecessarily. Walk briskly between ends.
😤
ComposureNever show frustration or temper on the green. Accept bad luck graciously. Your teammates and opponents will respect you for it.

🏠 Indoor vs Outdoor
Understanding both games and how they differ
🌿 Outdoor Lawn Bowls
Played on natural grass greens, typically from April to September in the UK. The traditional form of the game with varying conditions depending on weather, grass length and maintenance.
9-17 second greens Weather dependent Grass surface Summer season
🏠 Indoor Bowls
Played on carpet rinks in purpose-built indoor centres. Extremely fast and consistent surfaces. Lawn bowls is a worldwide sport — played and followed internationally across the UK, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, South Africa and beyond. Major tournaments are broadcast on dedicated World Bowls YouTube channels and Sky Sports.
14-20 second greens Year round Carpet surface Worldwide following

🌍 Bowls Around the World

Lawn bowls is a genuinely worldwide sport with a huge international following. It is particularly popular across the UK, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, South Africa, and many Commonwealth nations.

📺 Matches are broadcast on dedicated World Bowls YouTube channels and Sky Sports in the UK.

🏆 Major tournaments include:
🏆 World Indoor Bowls Championship
🏆 World Outdoor Bowls Championship
🏆 Commonwealth Games Bowls
🏆 UK Open and British Isles Championships

Prize money at professional level is substantial — indoor bowls in particular is a genuine professional sport with elite international players.
